• Blood tests like CBC (Complete Blood Count), KFT (Kidney Function Test), LFT (Liver
Function Test), HLA B27 (Human Leukocyte Antigen B27), Uric acid levels, Human
Immunodeficiency Virus, Rheumatoid factor, X-ray and Antinuclear antibodies, tests for
specific infections like streptococcal and staphylococcal antibodies can help differentiate
psoriasis from other conditions with similar symptoms.
• KOH preparation should be done to rule out fungal infection (Tinea etc) in cases of psoriasis
of hands, feet, and nails.

TEST DESCRIPTION
1) Bedside tests Two simple bedside tests were done to confirm the diagnosis.
a) Grattage test This is positive when scales are accentuated by grating lesions with a glass
slide.
b) Auspitz sign When gently scraping off all the scales from lesions with a glass slide, a
glistening white adherent membrane (Berkley’s membrane) appears. Once
this membrane is removed, punctate bleeding points become visible. (positive
Auspitz sign).
2) Skin biopsy Skin biopsy (done in doubtful cases) shows epidermis and dermal changes.
Epidermal changes include parakeratosis, absent granular layer, regular
acanthosis, suprapapillary thinning, elongation of rete ridges and collection
of neutrophils in the epidermis (spongiform pustule of Kogoj and Munro’s
microabscess). Dermal changes: dilatation and tortuosity of capillary loops in
dermal papillae, and lymphocyte infiltrates in the upper dermis.
