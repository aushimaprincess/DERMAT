Irritant Contact Dermatitis • Diagnosis by exclusion: history of repeated irritant exposure,
lesions confined to contact area, immediate burning more than
itching, improves on avoidance;
• No positive patch test.
Allergic Contact Dermatitis • Requires positive patch test (type IV hypersensitivity);
• Pruritic eczematous lesions often extending beyond contact site,
delayed onset (24–72h after exposure).
Seborrheic Dermatitis • Clinical diagnosis: chronic erythematous patches with greasy
yellow scales on seborrheic areas (scalp, eyebrows, nasolabial
folds, chest);
• Recurs with stress or winter.
Nummular Dermatitis • Circular or oval pruritic eczematous plaques on extremities,
frequently oozing;
• Diagnosis supported by negative KOH to rule out tinea.
Dyshidrotic Dermatitis • Characteristic presentation of recurrent deep-seated vesicles on
sides of fingers, palms, soles, intensely pruritic, often triggered by
stress or seasonal factors.
An accurate diagnosis is crucial in a disease condition to provide the optimum treatment. In
each case of Vicharchika, the Dosha condition has to be analysed along with identification
of the exact cause. Appropriate medicines are to be selected based on the cause and the
involvement of doshas. External applications are generally indicated if the Dosha is situated
in the Twak.
The acute stage is generally considered as a ‘Vata Paittika’ predominant condition, while the
sub-acute stage is ‘Pitta Kaphaja’, and the chronic stage is ‘Vata Kaphaja’. The chronic stage
may be considered as Kitibha rather than Vicharchika.
