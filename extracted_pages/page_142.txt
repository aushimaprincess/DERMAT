Updates in pathogenesis and its relevance 12, 13, 14
Various theories have been propounded for the causation of vitiligo which are listed in the box
below
1. Genetic predisposition
2. Autoimmune destruction of melanocytes.
3. Neural pathways (explains the stress and segmental type)
4. Metabolic abnormalities & oxidative stress - Accumulation of toxic metabolites and oxidants that
damage melanocytes.
5. Self destruction of melanocytes (autocyto destructive)
The effect of these factors is largely confined to autoimmune damage which has a close
relationship of specific immune cells and cytokines which are important as many new drugs
have been approved to target the specific cytokines which helps to prevent “by-stander cells”
and also helps to ensure targeted therapy
The autoimmune theory revolves around the role of CD8+ T-cells and T cells. The former

is a cell that causes cell damage and the latter is a memory cell that leads to relapses. In
fact, formal studies have shown that biopsies of vitiligo show melanocyte destruction with
presence of melanocyte specific CD8+T-cells as compared to controls. These T-cells result in
cell damage apoptosis via the release of IFN-γ which mediates it action via JAK/STAT pathway.
Etiological Factors:15
The etiological factors of Shvitra include the factors responsible for the causation of the
Samanya Kustha. They can be classified as
Aaharaja (Dietary factors):
• Intake of incompatible food combinations (Virudha Ahara) such as milk with fish
• Intake of unctuous and heavy-to-digest drinks (Snigdha-Guru Pana)
• Simultaneous intake of hot and cold potency foods and irregular fasting
• Overeating, eating uncooked food, or eating before previous meal is digested
• Excessive intake of freshly harvested grains, curd, fish, salt, and sour substances
• Excessive intake of black gram (Masha), radish (Mulaka), sesame (Tila), and jaggery
Viharaja (Lifestyle factors):
• Suppression of natural urges like vomiting
• Excessive physical exercise in hot weather or after a heavy meal
• Use of cold water immediately after exposure to scorching sun, exertion, or during a
frightening situation
• Sleeping during daytime
• Improper administration of Panchakarma therapies
