Correlation of Atopic Dermatitis, Nummular Eczema, Dyshidrotic Eczema, and Contact
Dermatitis with Vicharchika
In Ayurvedic classics, Vicharchika is a skin disorder characterized by Kandu or Atikandu
(itching), Pidika (papular eruptions), Shyava (blackish or greyish discoloration), Bahusrava
(excessive or copious discharge), Lasikadhya (excessive watery discharge), Atiruja (excessive
pain), and Raji (manifestation of linear lesions).
1. सकण्डूठः वप्डका श्ािा बहुस्ािा विचरचचिका||२६|| Cha. Chi. 7/26
2. राज्योऽवतकण्डिरतचिरुजठः सरूक्ा भिव्त गारितेषु विचरचचिका्ाम् ||१३|| Su.Ni. 5/13
3. सकण्डूवपटिका श्ािा लसीकाढ्ा विचरचचिका||१८|| A.H.Ni.14/18
According to Acharya Charaka, Vicharchika is considered to have a predominance of Kapha
Dosha. In general, skin disorders involve all three Doshas—Vata, Pitta, and Kapha. This is also
true for Vicharchika, which has involvement of all three Doshas.
The general features outlined in classical Ayurvedic texts may not be present at every stage of
the disease, as they depend on the involvement of the Doshas and the patient’s condition. Thus,
the symptoms of Vicharchika can be correlated with different types of dermatitis, depending
on their clinical presentation. It is also important to note that not every clinical presentation
will have a specific name, allowing physicians with the flexibility to differentiate and label the
condition as needed. For example, in the case of atopic dermatitis or nummular dermatitis,
the dry stage can be correlated with Vicharchika symptoms such as Kandu (itching), Atikandu
(intense itching), Pidaka (papular eruptions), and Shyava (blackish or greyish discoloration).
During this stage, oozing is typically absent. However, in the wet stage of the same disease,
Lasikadhya (excessive watery discharge) may be present.
Dyshidrotic Eczema (DE) may be correlated with Vicharchika with the involvement of Pitta along
with Vata and Kapha, as it commonly occurs in individuals with hyperhidrosis, and symptoms
such as burning sensation and redness are observed in some cases. The symptoms of Atikandu
(Severe itching), Lasika (fluid-filled vesicles), Pidaka are seen in DE patients. In cases of secondary
infections, symptoms mentioned in the classics such as Atiruja (excessive pain), Atisrava (Pus
discharge) may also be observed. Management for this condition should include Pittahara drugs
in addition to the general treatment of Vicharchika if associated with burning sensation and
hyperhidrosis. Daha, Paka, and Putigandha also indicates involvement of Pitta.
In contact dermatitis, symptoms are similar to Vicharchika like Kandu, Daha, Ruja, Srava, Sphota
etc. In addition to the general line of treatment, Garavisha Chikitsa may also be incorporated
