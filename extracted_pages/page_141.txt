Case Definition in Ayurveda:
Shvitra is characterized by whitish, de-pigmented areas without exudation and non-infectious
patches on the skin (अपटरस्ाविवितेतिणचिमं्डला)5. The term Shvitra is a combination of Shweta and
Rak.
Shweta means whiteness, and Rak is a suffix of ‘Shweta’Dhātu.TheNirukti of the term reveals
white colour or changes toward white colour.
Synonyms: Kilasa, Shweta, Shouklya, Aruna, and Daruna.
INTRODUCTION (Incidence/Prevalence, Morbidity/Mortality/Risk Factors)7, 8, 9, 10
• Vitiligo, a common depigmenting skin disorder, has an estimated prevalence of 0.5–2%
of the population worldwide. The prevalence of vitiligo is quite variable around the world,
being more frequent in Africa (0.4%), Europe (0.4%), and Oceania (1.2%), than in North
America (0.2%) and Asia (0.1%).
• In India, the reported prevalence of vitiligo ranges from approximately 0.5% to 2.5%, with
some regional studies indicating even higher figures in specific populations.
• There is no difference in prevalence between races or genders. Females typically acquire
the disease earlier than males. Onset may occur at any age, although about half of patients
will have vitiligo by 20 years of age.
• It is broadly classified into nonsegmental vitiligo (NSV) and segmental (SV), with NSV
being the most common form.
• Vitiligo significantly impacts patient’s psychological and social well-being.
In Ayurveda, nearly all skin conditions are classified generally as sub-varieties of Kustharoga
and come under the general categories of “Kustharogadhikara” while a few are also described
under “Kshudrarogadhikara”. However, among the different types of Kustha that are described
in Brihattrayi, Shvitra has not been named as a sub type. But due to the Vaisishthaya
(specificity) of the presentation, Shvitra is discussed separately in the Kustha chapter, which
gives it particular attention.11
