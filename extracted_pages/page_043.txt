Dyshidrotic Eczema:
Dyshidrotic eczema (DE) or acute palmoplantar eczema is a common cause of hand dermatitis
in adults. It is a vesiculobullous disorder of the hands and soles. It is an intraepidermal
spongiosis of the thick epidermis in which accumulation of edema causes the formation of
small, tense, clear, fluid-filled vesicles on the lateral aspects of the fingers that can become
large and form bullae.

Contact Eczema:
Contact dermatitis (CD) is an inflammatory skin condition triggered by chemicals or metal ions
that cause irritant effects, or by contact allergens that modify proteins and induce immune
responses, primarily through T cells. CD can manifest as irritant contact dermatitis or allergic
contact dermatitis, both of which can be acute or chronic.
Irritant contact dermatitis results from direct chemical damage and does not involve immune
reactions or prior sensitization, leading to similar responses among most individuals exposed
to irritants.
In contrast, allergic contact dermatitis is a delayed hypersensitivity reaction (type IV) that
requires prior exposure to allergens. Skin lesions from CD can occur due to various irritants
or allergens, UV radiation, microbial exposure, or intrinsic factors. When related to work
environments, it may be classified as occupational contact dermatitis, often due to substances
like alcohol-based disinfectants, detergents, or latex gloves. Parthenium dermatitis is a form
of allergic contact dermatitis (ACD) caused by airborne particles (especially sesquiterpene
lactones) from the weed Parthenium hysterophorus, leading to itchy rashes (papules/plaques)
on exposed skin (face, neck, chest, limbs).
