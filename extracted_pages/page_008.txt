FOREWORD
The standardization of clinical practice is fundamental to ensuring quality, safety, and consistency across
healthcare delivery systems. Recognizing this imperative, the Ayush Vertical, under the leadership and
guidance of the Directorate General of Health Services (DGHS), initiated the systematic development of
Standard Treatment Guidelines (STGs) for common clinical conditions across traditional systems of
medicine. Building upon the successful publication of our earlier volumes on Musculoskeletal and
Metabolic Disorders, I am pleased to present the third set in the series: Standard Treatment Guidelines
for Dermatological Disorders.
This volume addresses five commonly encountered clinical entities—Psoriasis, Dermatitis, Vitiligo,
Urticaria, and Dermatophytosis. These guidelines are designed to assist Ayush practitioners in delivering
standardized, evidence-informed, and safe clinical care while preserving the foundational diagnostic and
therapeutic principles of Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, and Homeopathy. Beyond routine clinical application,
this document aims to foster consistency in practice, enhance quality assurance, support capacity
building, and provide a framework for future epidemiological and clinical research.
The preparation of these guidelines underscores the steadfast commitment of the Ayush Vertical to
institutionalizing quality assurance within public health services. Developed through an extensive
collaborative mechanism, this document brings together technical expertise from the Ayush Research
Councils, senior academic specialists across all four traditional streams, and distinguished experts from
modern medical specialties. This interdisciplinary approach ensures that the protocols remain deeply
rooted in classical wisdom, scientifically robust, and aligned with modern public health standards.
A defining strength of this publication is its rigorous, field-level validation. To ensure operational
feasibility in real-world clinical environments, the Ayush Vertical, in active collaboration with the
Directorate of Ayush, Government of NCT of Delhi, conducted comprehensive field testing of the draft
protocols. Medical Officers engaged in primary and secondary healthcare delivery tested the guidelines
in their daily practice, providing critical operational feedback. Inclusion of their observations in the final
publication has significantly enhanced its clinical relevance and practical utility.
I place on record my sincere gratitude to the members of the National Expert Committee, the Editorial
Board, subject experts from the Ayush Research Councils, modern medicine specialists, the Directorate
of Ayush (NCT of Delhi), and all participating Medical Officers. Their technical rigor, dedication, and spirit
of collaboration have been crucial in bringing this project to fruition.
I am confident that the Standard Treatment Guidelines for Dermatological Disorders will serve as an
authoritative reference for clinicians, academic faculties, researchers, and public health administrators.
It is our earnest hope that this effort will further strengthen the delivery of accessible, safe, and quality-
driven Ayush healthcare services to the nation.
