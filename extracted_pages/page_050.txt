Referral Criteria
• Same as mentioned earlier at level 1
• Non-response to treatment after 4–6 weeks of individualized therapy.
• Complicated or severe forms (e.g., eczema herpeticum, generalized erythroderma)
• Requirement for skin biopsy or histopathological confirmation.
• Presence of psychological disturbance (e.g., anxiety, depression due to disfigurement)
• Immunocompromised states (e.g., HIV, chemotherapy) complicating presentation.
• Referral if lesions are widespread, unresponsive, or infected.
• Autoimmune disorders with cutaneous overlap (e.g., SLE, celiac disease).
• Need for multi-disciplinary care (e.g., dermatology, allergology, psychiatry).
• Any other complication considered life-threatening by the investigator/clinician.

Clinical Diagnosis
Along with the newly diagnosed cases, the cases diagnosed at levels 1 and 2 that have multi-
spectrum complications are addressed at the tertiary level. In level 3, the clinical diagnosis of
Vicharchika is made according to Ayurveda, focusing on the Doshas involved in the pathology
of the clinical manifestation. Although Ayurveda categorizes Vicharchika under the broad
term “Kustha Roga,” it recognizes different varieties based on specific clinical presentations.
Clinical Diagnosis will be the same as in level 1 and 2.
Confirmation of diagnosis and assessment of severity can be done with the help of investigations
such as
• Patch test (for allergic contact dermatitis)
• Detailed evaluation: Skin biopsy
• Peripheral blood eosinophil count.
The treatment strategy planning at level 3 includes purificatory therapies for Bahudosha
Kustha, and subsequent Rasayana medicines for strengthening the purified Dhathu. In the
case of Madhyama Dosha or Alpa Dosha Kustha, Samanoushadhi will be utilized as per Dosha
dominance and Dhatu involvement.
