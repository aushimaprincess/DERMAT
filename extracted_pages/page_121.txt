CLINICAL PRESENTATION10-14
• Lesion morphology: Wheals are abrupt, well-circumscribed, raised, itchy erythematous
to pale lesions varying from millimeters to several centimeters; they appear, migrate, and
typically resolve within twenty-four hours without residual marks. They cause intense
pruritus and/or burning sensations.
• Angioedema: Characterized by sudden swelling of deeper dermis, subcutaneous tissue, or
mucosa (e.g., lips, eyes, genitals); often painful (not pruritic), resolving within up to 72 hours
• Distribution & duration: Can affect any part of body, including scalp, palms, and soles.
• Associated symptoms: May include systemic manifestations like headache, dizziness,
throat tightness, wheezing, GI discomfort (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea), arthralgia, fever etc.
• Dermographism & physical triggers: Stroking skin may reproduce wheals
(“dermatographism”) within minutes; characteristic of physical urticaria-physical factors
(pressure, temperature changes, vibration, sunlight) may precipitate symptoms
• Alarming features indicating vasculitis or anaphylaxis: Wheals lasting more than 24
hours, painful, non-blanching, or leaving purpura/hyperpigmentation suggest urticarial
vasculitis. Associated symptoms such as hypotension, stridor, GI symptoms, or confusion
point toward systemic anaphylaxis.
