Psoriasis Differential
Characteristic and Diagnostic Features
Subtype Diagnosis
Secondary Generalized erythematous psoriasiform papules with collarette
syphilis scale; palms and soles affected; associated with nonscarring
alopecia, lymphadenopathy, systemic symptoms (malaise, fever,
headache, myalgias). Chancre may still be evident in early stages
of secondary syphilis; rapid plasma reagin confirms diagnosis.
Pityriasis rosea 1–2 weeks after a vague “viral” episode.
A single patch appears on the trunk, followed by smaller lesions
along skin cleavage lines. Usually self-limiting and does not
usually recur; associated with Herpesvirus 6; epidemics occur in
spring and fall.
Erythrodermic psoriasis Severely disabling Diffuse erythema >75% body surface area
with or without exfoliation; superficial desquamation, hair loss,
nail dystrophy.
Pityriasis rubra Orange-red plaques start on the head and spread caudally; small
pilaris areas of spared skin is frequently present; yellowish thickening
of palms and soles; nail changes similar to psoriasis.Skin biopsy
confirms diagnosis
Drug eruptions Generalized erythroderma develops approximately 7–10 days
after the initiation of a new drug. Skin biopsy confirms diagnosis;
eosinophilia is suggestive of a drug eruption.
Pustular psoriasis Individual/coalescing sterile pustules that occur either in an acute,
generalized fashion or are localized to palms and soles.Usually
affects aged >65 y; >25% of patients have a positive family
history.
Irritant Irritant dermatitis is most frequently caused by occupational
dermatitis exposure, either as an industrial contact or as a household
contact. Clinical features, ranging from a little dryness, redness
or chapping to various types of eczematous dermatitis to an acute
caustic burn.
Folliculitis Dome-shaped follicular pustules. Pseudofolliculitis seen in the
beard area (after shaving), thighs, and arms (after epilation by
waxing).
Allergic contact Develops due to involvement of immunological pathway, being
dermatitis a type IV (delayed hypersensi tivity) reaction to exogenous
contact antigens. Progresses from erythema to edema to papu
lovesiculation.
Inverse psoriasis Well-demarcated, erythematous lesions with minimal scaling.
Affects intertriginous Areas.
Intertrigo Moist, erythematous lesions of intertriginous areas, caused by
mechanical factors or secondary infection; difficult to distinguish
from inverse psoriasis
