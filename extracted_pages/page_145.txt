Important points of history that are helpful in the diagnosis of vitiligo include:
• Age at onset of lesions
• Factors or events that may cause the onset of vitiligo
• Symptoms associated with the lesions
• Progression or spread of lesions
• Changes observed in lesions over time
• Presence of concomitant diseases
• Current medications
• Occupational history/ exposure to chemicals or radiation
• Family history of vitiligo and autoimmune diseases
Physical Examination of vitiligo will include these features:
• The most affected areas include the face, neck, forearms, dorsal hands, fingers, feet, and
scalp. On the face, lesions typically show a periocular or perioral distribution.
• In cases of generalized involvement, lesions may also be seen on the areolae, nipples, and
genital region. Sites frequently exposed to friction or trauma- such as bony prominences
(elbows, knees) are often involved, which may reflect the Koebner phenomenon, observed
in 20–60% of vitiligo cases.
• Vitiligo manifests as depigmented macules or patches with a convex border without signs
of inflammation with normal skin around.
• Macules are milky-white in color, well-demarcated with any shape
• Ranges in size from a few millimeters to a few centimeters.
• Signs of other associated diseases may also be present like goiter, anemia, generalized
wasting, premature grey hairs, alopecia areata and weakness, etc
• Leukotrichia, or depigmentation of body hair within vitiliginous macules, may be noted
during examination and is considered a marker of poor response to repigmentation
therapies, as spontaneous recovery of pigment in affected hair is uncommon.
