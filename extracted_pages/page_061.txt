2. Region Specific Examination
Table 2:
Type Clinical Sign
Tinea corporis (body) Ringworm lesion on trunk/ limbs, raised scaly borders, presents as
circumscribed lesions with a wide variety of appearances, including
scales, vesicles, and pustules.
Tinea cruris (groin) Red itchy rash in groin/ thighs, often bilateral.
Tinea capitis (scalp) Patchy hair loss, inflammation, black dots, scaling, boggy swellings
(kerion), broken hair.
Tinea pedis (feet) Scaling, fissures between toes, moccasin – type dryness, cracked
skin between toes (itching and inflammation).
Tinea manuum (hands) Typically involves One hand, dry scaling, hyperkeratosis or
blistering.
Tinea unguium (nails) Thickened, brittle, discoloured nails, onycholysis.
Tinea faciei (face) Erythematous plagues, often aggravated by sun or steroids.
Tinea barbae (bearded area) Dermatophytosis of the bearded area, appears as a pustular
folliculitis.
