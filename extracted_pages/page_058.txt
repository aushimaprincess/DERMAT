CASE DEFINITION
Superficial infection caused by a dermatophyte is known as Dermatophytosis or ringworm
infection. This term is a misnomer because worms are not involved1. “Tinea”, the Latin name
for worm, describes the serpentine appearance of the skin lesions2.
As per Ayurveda, Dadru is characterised by Kanduh [associated with itching sensation],
Ragah [reddish nodules], Tamra VarnaPidka [copper coloured papule/eruptions], Udgata
Mandalam/UtsannaMandalata [elevated ring-shaped plaque], Dirgha Pratana [spreading
out], Durvavat[resembling Durva], Atasi Kusuma Chhavi [resembling the flower of Atasi],
Anushangi [chronic /associated over a long period]3
INTRODUCTION (incidence/prevalence /morbidity/mortality/risk factors)
• Dermatophytes (keratinophilic fungi) are the most common cutaneous fungal infections
seen in humans affecting skin, hairs and nails with a considerable morbidity.4
• According to World Health Organization (WHO), the prevalence rate of superficial mycotic
infection worldwide has been found to be 20-25%.
• Its prevalence varies in different countries. It is more prevalent in tropical and subtropical
countries like India where the heat and humidity are high for most part of the year.5,6
Community - based surveys and dermatology outpatient data show 20-30% prevalence,
especially in humid regions. In some areas, up to 50-60% of dermatology OPD cases are
due to dermatophytosis.
• An epidemic of recalcitrant dermatophyte infection has engulfed India and many part of the
world and the species that is dominant has been controversially named as Trichophyton
indotinae7. This is resistant to many front-line drugs. This has led to an alteration in the
immunity8. A National Task force was formed which details the cause of recalcitrant
infection as below. Notably steroid abuse has lead to varied forms of Tinea classically
tinea incognito and majocchi variant which do not respond to conventional agents.
