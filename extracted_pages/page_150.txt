DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA39
Vitiligo is typically diagnosed clinically, based on the presence of acquired, amelanotic,
non-scaly, chalky-white macules with well-defined margins. These lesions often follow
characteristic distribution patterns, including periorificial areas, lips, distal extremities, genitalia,
segmental zones, and sites of friction.
Confirmatory laboratory investigations are generally unnecessary, except when required to
rule out other differential diagnoses. Histopathological examination of the central portion of
a vitiligo lesion reveals complete loss of melanin pigment in the epidermis and an absence of
melanocytes. Occasionally, lymphocytic infiltration may be observed at the advancing margins
of the lesions, suggesting active disease progression.
