Dadru typically presents with red, circular patches on the skin that are raised and itchy. The
main symptoms include intense itching (kandu), reddish discoloration (raga), small eruptions
or papules (pidaka), and ring-shaped raised lesions (utsanna mandala Pari Mandala). These
lesions often spread gradually and may resemble the Flax flower (Atasi Pushpa) or Bermuda
Grass (Durva). In some cases, burning sensation, scaling, or oozing can occur.17,18
According to Dalhaṇa, Dadru is of two types: Sita (white), Asita (black)19. Asita Dadru is
considered difficult to treat due to its severity, deep tissue involvement, and chronic nature. In
contrast, Sita Dadru is easily curable, superficial, and causes milder symptoms. Dadru Kushtha
rarely has complications; improper treatment can lead to further Dosha Dushti (vitiation of
Dosha), causing increased Dhatu involvement and chronicity. Dadru Kushtha generally has
good prognosis.
CLINICAL EXAMINATION11
A thorough clinical examination helps to diagnose, classify and differentiate from other
dermatoses.
General Inspection
It includes observation of site(s) of infection, number, size and distribution of lesion. Common
sites include groin, axillae, trunk, face, feet, hands, nails, scalp. Ask about onset, progression,
recurrence, family history and steroid/ antifungal use.
