CASE DEFINITION
Urticaria (hives) is characterized by the rapid onset of lesions known as wheals that consist of
a central mid dermal swelling with or without surrounding erythema, with associated pruritus,
lasting anywhere from 1 to 24 hours. The term urticaria is named after the stinging nettle
plant (Urtica dioica), which contains histaminic acid. Associated angioedema can sometimes
be seen, characterized by swelling of the deeper dermis and subcutaneous tissue lasting up
to 72 hours. 1,2
Sheetapitta is a clinical condition characterized by the presence of elevated patches in the
skin resembling that produced by the sting of the wasp (Varaṭīdaṣṭasaṁsthāna sotha), and is
associated with itching (Kandu), pricking pain (Todabahulah) vomiting (Chhardi), fever (Jvara)
and burning sensation(Daha). The term Udarda is also used to denote a similar condition. In
Sheetapitta, Vata is more predominant while Udarda is a kapha dominant condition. Elevated
reddish, itchy, round patches in the skin appearing in Sisira ritu, due to kapha dosha kopa is
called Udarda.3
INTRODUCTION3-6(Incidence/ Prevalence, Morbidity/ Mortality/ Risk Factors)
Urticaria is a common skin disease. About 8–20% of individuals in the community experience
an attack of urticaria at least once in their lifetime. 4
It can be seen in all ages and sexes but is slightly more common in young adults. In 40–50% of
the patients, urticaria and angioedema are seen in combination, only urticaria or angioedema
is seen in 40% and 20% of the people, respectively.5
Urticaria is a mast cell–driven disorder characterized by the recurrent development of hives
(urticaria) and/or swelling (angioedema).4
Urticaria affects nearly 1% of the world population, with over 100 million new cases each
year.India lacks specific epidemiological studies but contributes heavily to total global burden.
Chronic cases appear commonly in adults, especially women. India bears a significant burden
of urticaria in absolute terms—accruing the highest number of Disability-Adjusted Life Year
(DALYs) globally.
In children (<14 years) at a tertiary hospital in Northern India, 57% had spontaneous urticaria;
about 12% had dermographism, 2% had cold-induced, 1.3% aquagenic, and 1.7% cholinergic
urticaria.6,7
