As pigment cells are only present deep in the hair follicles, treatments mostly try to get
melanocytes to divide and migrate into affected skin. The aims of vitiligo treatment are
repigmentation and stabilization of the depigmentation process. In addition, agents that help
to ameliorate the oxidative stress are a useful adjunct. The choice of therapy depends on the
extent, location, and activity of disease as well as the patient’s age, skin type and motivation
to undergo treatment. In general, a period of at least 2–3 months is required to determine
whether a particular treatment is effective. The areas of the body that typically have the best
response to medical therapy are the face, neck, midextremities and trunk, while the distal
extremities and lips are the most resistant to treatment.
Red Flag Signs
These signs should be assessed before initiating treatment.
• Rapidly spreading or widespread depigmentation – suggests active disease needing
early intervention.
• Poliosis (white hair in lesions) – Indicates deep melanocyte loss; often resistant to
treatment.
• Mucosal or genital involvement – Associated with higher psychosocial impact and poor
response.
• Onset after age 60 or in infants – Atypical; consider alternative diagnoses (e.g.,
lymphoma, post-inflammatory changes).
• Associated autoimmune diseases – Screen for thyroid, diabetes, etc.
(A) Prevention
The prevention of vitiligo involves a three-tiered approach: Primary, Secondary, and
Tertiary prevention.
• Primary prevention aims to reduce the risk of disease onset by avoiding known
triggers such as skin trauma, chemical exposure, and excessive sunburn, while
promoting immune health through stress management, a balanced antioxidant-rich
diet, and healthy lifestyle practices.
• Secondary prevention focuses on early identification and prompt treatment to
prevent disease progression, along with sun protection and regular monitoring using
validated tools like the VIDA score.
• Tertiary prevention is directed toward managing established vitiligo, enhancing
repigmentation through individualized therapy, addressing psychosocial concerns
with counseling, improving cosmetic appearance, and monitoring for associated
autoimmune conditions.
