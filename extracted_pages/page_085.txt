In Ayurveda, psoriasis corresponds broadly to Kustha, a term for chronic skin diseases caused
by dosha-dhatu-mala imbalances and systemic disturbances [10]. Psoriasis aligns with subtypes
such as EkaKustha, Kitibha, Sidhma, Vipadika, Mandala, pundarika and Carmadala, involving
predominance of Vata-Kapha in plaque types, Pitta-Kapha in pustular forms, and Vata-Pitta
in psoriatic arthritis [11]. The pathogenesis (Samprapti) includes dietary and lifestyle factors
causing impaired digestion (Agni), accumulation of toxins (Ama), and obstruction of channels
(Srotorodha), paralleling immune dysregulation and systemic inflammation [12].
Ayurveda also recognizes the systemic nature of psoriasis through its holistic view of Kustha,
linking it to chronic metabolic and psychological imbalances. Comorbidities like cardiovascular
disorders, metabolic syndrome, and depression are conceptualized as manifestations of
Dushivisha (chronic toxins), Mandagni (impaired digestive fire), and Avarana Vata (obstruction
in channels), emphasizing the need for comprehensive management addressing physical and
mental health [13,14].This integrated understanding supports the use of Shodhana (detoxification),
Shamana (palliative treatment), Rasayana (rejuvenation), and Satvavajaya (psychological
therapies) to address both cutaneous lesions and systemic complications effectively [15].
