• The new species Trichophyton Indotinea has unique mechanisms of resistance and is fast

• Chronic and recurrent cases have increased significantly in the last 5-10 years due to
overuse of topical steroids and antifungal creams which have made them resistant to
common antifungal drugs.
• Dermatophytosis is not life threatening, but it causes severe itching and discomfort,
social embarrassment and stigma, sleep disturbances, work absenteeism and reduced
productivity.
• Study using DLQI (Dermatology Life Quality Index) shows moderate to severe impact on
quality of life in 40-60% of chronic/ recurrent cases. Psychological stress, anxiety and low
self-esteem are common.10,11
All skin diseases in Ayurveda have been classified under the broad heading of “Kustha”
which are further categorized in to Mahakustha &Kshudrakustha12. As per Acharyas, Dadru
is a skin disease which is characterized by circular erythematous patches (Mandala), itching
(Kandu), burning sensation (Daha), redness (Raga), and eruption of elevated lesions (Utsanna
Mandala)- which mirror the clinical features of Tinea corporis and cruris. The Sthana (site of
occurrence) of the Dadrukustha is fourth layer of skin and the doshic predominance is kapha
pitta.13 Acharya Charaka included Dadru among kshudrakustha while Acharya Susrutha has
classified Dadru among Mahakusthas, Acharya Vagbhata highlighted the Anushangi nature
of Dadrukustha and the spread resembling Durva grass. The symptoms of Daha (Burning
sensation) and Srava (discharge) are also associated symptoms of the disease.

Typical Lesion-
• A typical lesion of dermatophytosis is an annular or arcuate plaque which spreads
centrifugally (away from the centre).
• The edge is active, showing papulo-vesiculation, pustulation and scaling, while the centre
is usually relatively clear.
• In chronic lesion there may be nodules, hyperpigmentation and even lichenification
(thickening, hyperpigmentation and increased skin markings) in the centre.
