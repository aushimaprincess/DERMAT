CASE DEFINITION
Dermatitis refers to inflammation of the skin resulting in erythema, itching, edema, and
vesiculation, often followed by crusting and scaling. It includes a broad spectrum of disorders
such as atopic dermatitis, contact dermatitis (irritant/allergic), seborrheic dermatitis, nummular
dermatitis and dyshidrotic dermatitis.1,2
Vicharchika is one among the eighteen types of integumentary diseases enlisted in the
classical textbooks of Ayurveda. It is classified as a Ksudra Kustha, ie minor skin disease. This
skin disorder is characterized by mild to intense itching, papular eruptions, blackish/greyish
discoloration and excessive discharge with the predominant involvement of Kapha Dosha in
general.3,4
INTRODUCTION (Incidence/Prevalence, Morbidity/Mortality/Risk Factors)1,25,6,7,8,9,10,11,12
• Atopic dermatitis affects up to 20% of children and around 10% of adults globally, with
increasing prevalence in industrialized nations.
• Contact dermatitis accounts for nearly 70% of occupational skin diseases worldwide.
• Seborrheic dermatitis is estimated to affect 1–5% of the general population but may
exceed 85% in HIV-infected individuals.
• Nummular dermatitis represents approximately 0.2–0.5% of dermatology cases, while
dyshidrotic dermatitis constitutes 5–20% of hand eczema cases globally.
• In India, similar prevalence patterns are observed, with a significant burden of atopic and
contact dermatitis, particularly among industrial workers and urban populations.
