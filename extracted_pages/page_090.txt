Types of Psoriasis Description Figure
Nail psoriasis • Nail dystrophy may be seen in all types
(psoriatic of psoriasis or occasionally as an isolated
onychodystrophy) feature presented in 40 % of cases at
any time, and the lifetime prevalence is
significantly higher.
• Nail changes includes pitting, separation
of nail plate from nail bed appearing as
yellowish discolred nails (onycholysis)
and thickened, irregular, brownish nails
(onychodystrophy)
Mucosal lesions • Mucosal involvement is infrequent but
presents as a geographical tongue (benign
migratory glossitis) and fissured tongue.
Non-pustular • Commonly seen on palms and soles,
palmoplantar presented as typical scaly patches with fine
psoriasis silvery scales, can be evoked on scratching.
However, on the wrist, forearm or palm, there
is a clear edge without vesiculation
Ocular lesions • Psoriasis may affect ocular structure
directly or indirectly and is associated with
immunological phenomena.
• Directly affected by blepharitis, chronic
nonspecific conjunctivitis, xerosis, and rarely
keratitis.
• Immunologically affected uveitis is associated
with extensive psoriasis and psoriatic
arthritis.
Extra-Cutaneous Manifestions
Psoriatic arthritis • There can be involvement of joints of hands
and feet especially distal interphalangeal
joints.
• In some cases, spondylitis or sacro-iloitis can
also be seen. The arthritis can be deforming
at times.
